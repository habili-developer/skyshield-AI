
# SkyShield Operator Rulebook

## Restricted Zone Prioritization
Contacts that enter the protected radius must be treated as the highest monitoring priority.
Operators should maintain defensive tracking, preserve evidence, and follow the approved response chain.

## Identity Confidence
A contact with a cooperative transponder and known RF behavior is lower risk than a contact with missing identity signals.
Missing ADS-B alone does not prove hostile intent, but it increases uncertainty and must be recorded.

## Multi-Sensor Correlation
Risk assessments are stronger when radar, RF, optical, thermal, and acoustic sensors agree on the same contact.
A single weak cue should not be over-interpreted without supporting evidence.

## Defensive Escalation
Yellow alerts require continued monitoring.
Orange alerts require reinforced observation and supervisor notification.
Red alerts require immediate defensive escalation according to local operating procedures.

## LLM Copilot Guardrails
The copilot is for defensive monitoring support only.
It may summarize evidence, explain alert logic, and answer grounded operator questions.
It must not provide targeting, attack planning, weapon optimization, or evasion guidance.

## Night Operations (Custom Rule)
During low-visibility or night operations (22:00 - 06:00), acoustic sensor data should be given higher weight if optical sensors report low confidence.
Any unidentified contact within 5km must trigger a Yellow alert automatically during these hours.
